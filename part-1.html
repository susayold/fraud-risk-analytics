THE RISK PROBLEM

Fraud detection is not a ranking problem alone.

A fraud model can assign higher scores to suspicious transactions, but risk management requires a second layer of reasoning: which transactions should be allowed, reviewed, or blocked, and how much operational friction is acceptable?

Capture Fraud

Identify a disproportionate share of fraudulent activity within a highly imbalanced population.

Fraud Capture · Amount Capture · Top-K Capture

Control False Positives

Avoid unnecessary intervention on legitimate transactions while preserving useful coverage.

Precision · Legitimate Intervention · Legitimate Block

Respect Review Capacity

Manual investigation is finite. Review decisions operate inside predefined capacity.

Review Rate · Capacity Utilization · Overflow

Govern Change Over Time

Monitor data, scores, customer behavior, policy outcomes, and graph structure as they change.

Drift · Calibration · Ranking · Policy Stability
Decision Objective

Convert transaction risk into consistent ALLOW / REVIEW / BLOCK actions while balancing fraud protection, customer friction, review capacity, and explicitly simulated economics.

DECISION INTELLIGENCE

A score only becomes useful when it changes an action.

The fraud score determines risk ordering. The policy layer determines intervention under explicit constraints.

ALLOWLow intervention priorityTransaction proceeds under the frozen policy.
REVIEWAnalyst investigationCompetes for a finite queue · 0.50% capacity
BLOCKHighest-risk policy actionEligible only under the frozen block rule.
Graph Context Cannot Auto-BLOCKNetwork novelty may support REVIEW prioritization, but graph evidence alone cannot directly trigger BLOCK.
TUNE→CONFIRM→FREEZE→FINAL OOT→BOOTSTRAP→LOCK
Final OOT is evidence, not a tuning set.

Every public-facing metric belongs to an explicit evidence class.
